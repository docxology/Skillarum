*First, there is a possible repair of the common twenty-four-element
structure.*

Consider triples ((v,e,f)) consisting of a cube vertex, edge, and face such
that:

   -

   (v) is an endpoint of (e);
   -

   (v) lies on (f);
   -

   (e) is *not* contained in (f).

Call these *transverse triples*. At each vertex, each of its three edges
selects the unique face through that vertex that does not contain that edge.

There are exactly twenty-four such triples. Projection onto vertices,
edges, and faces gives respectively:

[
24=8\times3=12\times2=6\times4.
]

Now these really are three counts of *one explicitly defined set*.
Moreover, any compatible pair of components determines the third. I have
independently enumerated the triples and checked these properties with a
short Python calculation.

This does not vindicate my original statement about ordinary incidences. It
supplies a different construction that might serve the intended purpose.

It also offers a sharper test for the language proposal: can the
philosophical transitions be modeled by completing these triples? The
compatibility conditions and the framing role of the third component would
have to match Andrius’s definitions. Equal cardinalities alone would not
suffice.

*Second, I would like to clarify the scope of your triality claim in point
6.*

The standard complex exterior-algebra construction gives the vector
representation and the two half-spin representations as

[
V=W\oplus W^*,\qquad
S_{\mathrm{odd}}=\Lambda^{\mathrm{odd}}W,\qquad
S_{\mathrm{even}}=\Lambda^{\mathrm{even}}W,
]

with (\dim_{\mathbb C}W=4). These constructions and the triality
permutation of the three representation types are standard; Peter Woit’s notes
on spinor representations <https://www.math.columbia.edu/~woit/notes19.pdf>
provide a useful reference.

Here is my calculation from that model. Choose one splitting (W=L\oplus U),
with dimensions (1+3), and let a common (SL(3,\mathbb C)) subgroup act on
(U) while fixing (L). Then we can retain the following invariant groupings
simultaneously:

[
\begin{aligned}
V&=W\oplus W^* &&(4+4),\
S_{\mathrm{odd}}
&=L\oplus U\oplus(L\otimes\Lambda^2U)\oplus\Lambda^3U
&&(1+3+3+1),\
S_{\mathrm{even}}
&=\Lambda^0W\oplus\Lambda^2W\oplus\Lambda^4W
&&(1+6+1).
\end{aligned}
]

However, these are *selected groupings of invariant subspaces*, not three
different complete irreducible branching patterns. Under that subgroup, all
three representations refine to two singlets, a (3), and its dual.

Consequently, I would not yet accept an unrestricted impossibility
statement. If point 6 concerns complete irreducible branching under one
subgroup, that is a stronger and different requirement. Could we state
precisely which requirement was tested?

This construction still does not establish the theological identification,
its uniqueness, or compatibility of the selected groupings with triality.
Those remain substantive questions.
